[Back Through Time] ③ Seok-am Lee Dong-beom — "Jinsa Lee" Reread Through the Official Gazette

0001①②③④⑤⑥⑦

Before we begin

The third detailed chronology is about my great-great-grandfather Seok-am Lee Dong-beom (石庵 李東範, 1869–1940). He is known as "Jinsa Lee" in Baekbeom Ilji, and I introduced him once in Part 5 of the earlier series, "Seok-am Lee Dong-beom — The Life of 'Jinsa Lee.'"

When I wrote that part, I followed the career described in a 1917 newspaper article. Since then, as Official Gazette records were added to the Archive one by one, dates began to attach themselves to the career the newspaper had summed up in a single line. This part brings those dates together.

Sources: [G] government and public records (including the family register) · [F] originals and objects held by the family · [Gen] genealogy · [N] newspapers of the time · [L] later compilations, memoirs and survey reports (including Baekbeom Ilji) · [O] oral family tradition

Baekbeom Ilji is the memoir written by Kim Gu himself, and it is the most direct record of his 1898 stay in Hampyeong and the 1946 reunion, written by the person who lived them. Because it was written years after the events, names and dates in it are read against other records.

2. What the records show

Of the seven generations, my great-great-grandfather's chronology is the one most densely covered by government records. Almost every step of his official career from 1897 to 1903 carries an Official Gazette date.

Set out in order, these dates reveal a few things. He resigned as chambong of Jogyeongmyo after six days and as Jungchuwon councillor after one day, both at his own request. He served a little over a year as magistrate of Mangyeong and was appointed magistrate of Eunyul the very day after resigning. His name also appeared in the assessment of magistrates in February 1903.

The 1899 notice is a small but important record. That he was already called "former chambong" that year shows his official career began earlier than a later survey report, which dates his chambong post to 1902.

3. What the records do not show

Senior third rank (tongjeong daebu): Joseon Sinsa Daedongbo (1913), the 1917 newspaper and the genealogy all state that he rose to the senior third rank. Whether the 1902 Official Gazette record that seems to support this concerns him, and how the rank was conferred, are still being verified. The Gazette record in question is described in the footnote to Archive Record 003.

After Eunyul: How he left office and returned home after taking up the Eunyul post is still under review in some records. The 1917 newspaper says he gave up office to care for his parents.

The title "Jinsa": Baekbeom Ilji calls him "Jinsa Lee," but we have not found a record that he passed the jinsa examination.

Work at home: We have not yet confirmed the nature and dates of the financial cooperative founding committee and school principal roles mentioned in the 1917 newspaper, nor found primary records such as name boards or commemorative texts directly showing the building of the Sesimjeong and Yeonjeong.

"3,000 seok": How far the size of the estate reported in a later survey report is supported by records was examined separately in Series Part 10.

4. Host in 1898

According to the confirmed Official Gazette records, 1898, when Kim Gu stayed in this house, falls between July 1897, when my great-great-grandfather resigned as chambong of Jogyeongmyo, and April 1902, when he was appointed Jungchuwon councillor. We have not yet found records showing whether he held office in between. The story of those two weeks is in Series Part 2, and the story of the two sons meeting Kim Gu again half a century later is in Series Part 3.

My great-great-grandfather died in 1940. By 1946, when Kim Gu tried to find the house of his old benefactor again, he was no longer alive.

Closing

When I wrote Part 5, a single paragraph of the 1917 newspaper was almost all we had of his official career. This time the Official Gazette dates stand in line behind that paragraph. More dates do not mean every question is answered, however. How the rank was conferred and how he left office are still being checked against the records.

The next part looks at the chronology of his father, my fifth-generation ancestor Lee Gye-song (Hong-gyu).
