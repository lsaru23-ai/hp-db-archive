Pausing for Breath — After Ten Parts

The Lee Dong-beom Family of Hampyeong: A 128-Year Bond | Series Notice

[EN] English edition · Translated from the Korean original · 한국어 원문

For the past several days I have been running at a breathless pace. Beginning with the bond formed between Kim Gu and the "Jinsa Lee" of Hampyeong, and moving through Seok-am Lee Dong-beom, Chun-gang Lee Jae-seung, and the story of Lee Sang-rae and Shin Mun-ran, ten pieces have somehow accumulated.

At first it was simply a wish to put in order, little by little, the stories handed down in our family. But the more I laid out old photographs and newspapers, genealogies and maps one by one, the more unknown facts came to light, and each record led to another question.

My heartfelt thanks to everyone who has read these modest pieces to the end and offered encouragement. I was able to come this far thanks to the interest you showed each time a new part was posted.

1. Why I Am Slowing Down

This series has been written from the beginning on the principle of "writing only as much as the records confirm." Having moved quickly, I felt I needed time to stop and look back, if only to keep to that principle.

In fact, during the series I corrected the wording of the lineage in Part 7 after comparing it again with the original genealogy page, and while preparing Part 9 I also confirmed that the material we had been looking at was not a land ledger but an original cadastral map. It was a time of learning that even among stories I thought I knew well, there are parts that need to be checked again.

2. What I Will Do During the Break

I plan to compare again the newspaper originals and old documents collected so far, and to check years, names, and lineages. I will also identify the people and places in old photographs more accurately, and if there is anything to correct, I will make it public together with a record of the revision.

In particular, I intend to keep tracing what happened after the 1909 Righteous Army demand for 10,000 won in military funds, as well as the land records of Hampyeong and Muan. I will also continue the work of organizing confirmed facts, memories handed down in the family, and unresolved questions separately.

3. Stories I Will Tell When I Return

When I return, I would also like to tell somewhat different stories. There is the story of Lee Sang-rae and Shin Mun-ran, who, according to family tradition, met at Daejeon Station while studying separately in Seoul and Gwangju, and the story of family members who loved painting during the Japanese colonial period.

There are also the stories of the women who lived through the same era at Gwangju Girls' School, and of my great-uncle Lee Jae-hyeok and his house. Much still remains as family memory, so I intend to introduce them step by step after looking further into the materials.

If the ten parts so far have been a process of finding out "who they were," from now on I would also like to approach the question of "with what feelings, and in what daily lives, did they live?" If Part One was a story of people and sources, Part Two will be a story of lives and memories.

Rather than rushing to an answer, I will leave as accurate a record as can be confirmed, even if it takes a little longer.

Thank you for reading along. After putting the records in order for a while, I will return with deeper stories.

From a descendant of the Lee Dong-beom family of Hampyeong
