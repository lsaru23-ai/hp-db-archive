[Back Through Time] How We Correct the Record — Why We Read the Sources Again

0001①②③④⑤⑥⑦

Before we begin

Over the past few months this blog has published, one at a time, entries from the Official Gazette (Gwanbo), period newspapers and the Seungjeongwon Ilgi concerning my great-great-grandfather Seok-am Lee Dong-beom and other elders of the family. Each time we go back to an original page, there are moments when something we wrote earlier has to be corrected. Sometimes a single character was misread; sometimes one line of the genealogy was read too broadly.

Before starting the new series, "Back Through Time: Seven Generations of the Lee Dong-beom Family of Hampyeong," I want to set out the principles we follow in writing these records and how we have corrected them. Every part of the series follows the same principles.

1. Five principles for handling records

We keep original text, reading and interpretation apart. What is written on the page, how we read it, and what we take it to mean are never mixed in one sentence. Characters we cannot read are left as □.

We distinguish kinds of records. Records made by the state at the time (the Official Gazette, the Seungjeongwon Ilgi), documents held by the family, the genealogy, and later newspapers and biographical compilations carry different weight. The timelines mark which is which.

We never turn "no record found" into "it did not happen." What we could not find, we say we could not find. An office missing from the genealogy does not mean there was no office.

When we correct, we leave a trace. A misread character or a wrong statement is corrected together with what was changed and why. A correction that hides what was wrong cannot prevent the same mistake next time.

Some things are not published. We do not publish the lot number of the house, genealogy lineage pages, photographs of documents held by the family, or the location of graves. Records still being checked, or whose publication scope has not been decided, are marked only as "under review."

2. Records we actually corrected

① "Second," not "third" — reading a military examination diploma

敎旨 閑良李弘奎武科乙科第二人及第出身者

What we corrected

We first read the rank on the military examination diploma (hongpae) of my fifth-generation ancestor Lee Hong-gyu (Gye-song) as "third." Comparing the front of the diploma with the folded title strip kept with it, both read 第二人, "second." We corrected it to "second place in the eulgwa class."

What we still do not know

The diploma alone cannot confirm the formal name of the examination or the overall ranking. It needs to be checked against the list of successful candidates (bangmok).

The diploma is held by the family, so we give only the reading, not a photograph.

② Who composed the tomb inscription, and who built the pavilion — rereading one line of the genealogy

孫啓松撰碣 曾孫郡守東範建閣

What we corrected

We once wrote that Seok-am Lee Dong-beom composed the tomb inscription (myogal) of my seventh-generation ancestor Lee Don-hwa. Read again, this line of the genealogy says the inscription was composed by the grandson Gye-song (Lee Hong-gyu), and the great-grandson, Magistrate Dong-beom, built the stele pavilion (bigak). We had confused the author with the builder.

What we still do not know

We have not yet seen the inscription stone itself or established when the pavilion was built.

③ A character we could not read — "經" in the 1938 Dong-A Ilbo

古道에充實 春崗 李載昇 氏 / 咸平面長을 經하신 明哲한氏는 度量無限하나 …

What we corrected

This is the profile of my great-grandfather Chun-gang Lee Jae-seung on page 4 of the Dong-A Ilbo of 4 October 1938. When Record 011 in the Archive was first published, one character in this phrase could not be read and was left as "□하신(?)". Rechecking the original page, we read it as 經 ("to have served as"), and at the same time corrected "度量無限하고" to "度量無限하나" and "前年에 氏의" to "前年 氏의." The published Record 011 has carried the corrected reading since 20 November 2026.

What we keep separate

That the newspaper introduces him as "former head of Hampyeong township" is a different matter from his service being confirmed in official records, with dates. We have not yet found a personnel record showing when or for how long he served, and we are still comparing official and local-history sources. Not having found the record does not mean he did not serve.

So the chronology says

"Introduced by a 1938 newspaper as a former head of Hampyeong township (dates of service not confirmed by records)."

④ Names written in another book — 「在昇·在赫」 in Baekbeom Ilji

What we sorted out

The original text of Baekbeom Ilji, the memoir of Kim Gu, writes the names of the two sons of the Jinsa Lee family of Hampyeong as 「在昇·在赫」. The family genealogy and the family's own usage give 「載昇·載爀」. The series quotes the original as written, notes in a footnote that it differs from the family's spelling, and compares it with the spelling in the 1917 Maeil Sinbo. Which spelling was the official form of the names at the time still needs to be confirmed separately.

In addition

The 1917 Maeil Sinbo profile also prints the two sons' names as 「李載昇」 and 「李載爀」. A newspaper spelling, however, does not confirm the spelling in the family register.

3. A record we decided not to change — what rechecking showed

Correcting the record also includes rechecking and concluding that "no change is needed."

What we rechecked

The footnote to Record 003, on the 1902 Official Gazette, states that Gazette No. 2319 carries both an appointment notice raising "六品 李東範" to the senior third rank and an erratum. We checked whether this footnote claims more than the original page shows.

What we confirmed

We compared pages 1 and 2 of No. 2319 again. The eight men raised to the senior third rank as of 9 January in the page-1 notice and the eight men listed for promotion in rank (gaja) in the page-2 erratum match in name, rank and order.

Why we did not change it

The footnote says only that the two notices "appear together"; it does not assert how they are related or why the promotion was made. That is within what the page shows, so we left it as it is. What the promotion rewarded cannot be established from the page alone, so we do not state it.

What we still do not know

Whether this "六品 李東範" is the same person as Seok-am Lee Dong-beom is still being checked. The Gazette of April of the same year lists him as "九品," and we have not yet judged how the two records relate.

4. How we handle questions that remain after a correction

Some records still have parts we cannot settle even after correcting them. We do not rush to a conclusion; we note what has not been confirmed.

前參奉李東範氏가 … 姓名章을見失 … 該日븟터其圖章은施行치아니ᄒᆞᄋᆞᆸ

This is a notice on page 3 of the Hwangseong Sinmun of 23 August 1899, in which former chambong (參奉, a junior post at a royal shrine or tomb) Lee Dong-beom announced that he had lost his name seal. When Record 002 was first published, the last syllable of the notice was transcribed as 「ᄒᆞᆷ」. Enlarging the page and comparing other characters in the same notice, the final consonant turned out to be ㅂ, not ㅁ. On 20 November 2026 we therefore changed Record 002 in both the blog Archive and the online archive to 「ᄒᆞᄋᆞᆸ」, with a note that it had been corrected.

The question that remains

Because the ink has spread, the original page alone cannot settle whether the middle vowel of this syllable is arae-a (ㆍ) or ㅡ. For now we write 「ᄒᆞᄋᆞᆸ」, following a comparison with the arae-a of 「ᄒᆞ」 in the same notice. After checking how the same closing formula was printed in the Hwangseong Sinmun of the period, we will correct it again if needed and record the date and the grounds.

5. How the series is changing

Until now we posted each record as we found it. As the records accumulated, the life of each person began to come into view, so we are reorganizing the series.

New series title: "Back Through Time: Seven Generations of the Lee Dong-beom Family of Hampyeong"

Reading order: First comes a short chronology covering all seven generations in one post, in chronological order from the earliest, Lee Bung-seo, to Lee Sang-rae. The detailed chronologies then go the other way, starting from the nearest generation and moving back one person at a time.

① Lee Sang-rae → ② Chun-gang Lee Jae-seung (with his younger brother Lee Jae-hyeok) → ③ Seok-am Lee Dong-beom → ④ Lee Hong-gyu (Gye-song) → ⑤ Lee Gyeong-heon → ⑥ Lee Don-hwa → ⑦ Lee Bung-seo

Existing posts stay where they are: The ten main parts of the earlier series, the hiatus notice and the three extra parts remain in place.

Record entries have moved to the Archive: The record entries posted one by one have not been deleted but moved to the "Archive" category. Their addresses have not changed, so links already shared still open. Each part of the chronology will link to the original text and readings in the Archive as its sources.

How corrections are recorded: From now on, when an Archive entry is corrected, a "Correction record" will be added at its end, giving the date, the wording before and after, the grounds, and the status (confirmed / under further review). Earlier versions are never overwritten without a trace.

Closing

A record is not finished once it is written; it becomes a little more accurate each time we look at the original again and compare it with other sources. We will continue to record newly confirmed facts and corrections together with their grounds.

From here we move beyond introducing records one sheet at a time, to following the lives of the people within them. Starting from the nearest generation, Lee Sang-rae, and moving through Lee Jae-seung and Lee Dong-beom back to Lee Bung-seo, we will trace seven generations. The new series is called "Back Through Time: Seven Generations of the Lee Dong-beom Family of Hampyeong."
