The Head Family House and the Lee Jae-hyeok House — 128 Years in Two Homes

The Lee Dong-beom Family of Hampyeong: A 128-Year Bond | Part 4

[EN] English edition · Translated from the Korean original · 한국어 원문

In 1898, the young Baekbeom Kim Koo (also romanized as Kim Gu) stayed for about two weeks at the house of "Jinsa Lee" in Hampyeong. In the previous three parts, I introduced the bond between Kim Koo and Seok-am Lee Dong-beom recorded in Baekbeom Ilji, the family's customs and the story of his hiding, and the bond renewed after liberation together with the Gwangmyeong (光明) calligraphy.

This time I would like to look at the two houses of the Lee Dong-beom family of Hampyeong that were the setting of that story: the head family house that passed to Lee Dong-beom's eldest son, Lee Jae-seung, and the house of his second son, Lee Jae-hyeok. The two houses have come down within the same family, but today they stand in different historical situations.

One is the existing head family house that descendants have kept for many years; the other is the Lee Jae-hyeok House, known as a designated cultural heritage. What my father and I considered especially important in this research was to clarify accurately the relationship between these two houses.

1. Lee Dong-beom and His Two Sons

Seok-am Lee Dong-beom (石庵 李東範, 1869–1940), introduced in Part 1, was presented as a notable figure of South Jeolla Province in the Maeil Sinbo of July 8, 1917, and that article also records his family relationships. Lee Dong-beom's eldest son was Lee Jae-seung (李載昇, 1890–1962), and his second son was Lee Jae-hyeok (李載赫). These relationships can be confirmed through the Hampyeong Lee Clan Comprehensive Genealogy kept in our family and the 1917 newspaper article.

The house that continued through the line of the eldest son, Lee Jae-seung, is our head family house; the house known by the name of the second son, Lee Jae-hyeok, is now managed as a cultural heritage.

Our family line runs from Lee Dong-beom through Lee Jae-seung and my grandfather Lee Sang-rae (李祥來, 1909–1970) to my father, Lee Il-wan. I am a descendant of the next generation.

2. The Head Family House That Still Stands

In Hampyeong-ri, Hampyeong-eup, the main building of the head family house of the Lee Dong-beom and Lee Jae-seung line still stands today. It is also the house my father has kept for many years, and the recent Kim Koo Museum & Library interview was held there. Watching my father sit on the wooden floor of the old house and tell the stories of Kim Koo and our ancestors, I felt anew how important it is that the building still remains.

A house is not simply a space where people live. It is also a place where the memories of many generations accumulate and where a family's life and history continue. Our head family house is especially meaningful in that the descendants of the "Jinsa Lee" who appears in Baekbeom Ilji have kept it to this day. One thing, however, must be distinguished precisely here.

Whether the present main building of the head family house is the same building in which Kim Koo stayed in 1898 has not yet been confirmed.

The building's construction date, its history of extensions and alterations, and the history of its main timbers and structure must be confirmed through professional research in the future. The hexagonal pavilion and the earthen dugout that appear in Baekbeom Ilji, and the family story of hiding in the attic room, likewise need to be examined by distinguishing the spaces of that time from the present buildings.

Our family treasures the fact that the head family house still exists, but we do not intend to claim on that basis alone that the building of 128 years ago remains exactly as it was. Rather, we want to clarify its history by carefully comparing the building that remains today with the old records.

3. The 2003 Report

Meanwhile, at 379-2 Hampyeong-ri stands a house known by the name of Lee Dong-beom's second son, Lee Jae-hyeok. In December 2003, the "Survey Report on the Lee Jae-hyeok House, Hampyeong" was prepared for review by the South Jeolla Provincial Cultural Heritage Committee, and on that basis the house was designated a cultural heritage of South Jeolla Province on February 13, 2004. This report is an important source that surveyed the architectural features and history of the house.

The report states that this house had been known as the "Lee Jinsa house" (李進士家) and that it was built by Lee Dong-beom. It also describes the present Lee Jae-hyeok House as a building that was "originally the sarangchae" (the men's quarters), suggesting that at the time several buildings formed a single compound.

In particular, the report records that the existing sarangchae was built in 1917 and the gate building in 1929. These dates are later than Kim Koo's stay in Hampyeong in 1898. Therefore, the buildings of the Lee Jae-hyeok House confirmed today should not all be regarded as buildings from the time Kim Koo stayed there. The report also states that the attic room where Kim Koo is said to have stayed was in "the seven-bay main house," and that this main house was torn down and sold off.

Here, however, there is something that requires caution.

The report does not specify exactly where the demolished seven-bay main house stood or which building it was, and the main building of the head family house of the Lee Jae-seung line that remains today was not included in the scope of the report's survey.

The address of the property surveyed in the report is 379-2 Hampyeong-ri, and the main building of our head family house stands on a different lot. Of course, the fact that the lot numbers differ does not by itself prove that the two buildings were separate from the beginning, since the land may have been subdivided later. For the same reason, however, the demolition of the main house recorded in the report cannot simply be equated with the loss of the main building of the head family house that remains today.

Where the demolished main house stood, how the two houses were arranged in relation to each other in the past, and how the land was subdivided and passed down all require further research. What we want to clarify is not simply which house is older. It is to understand accurately how the two houses related to each other and changed within one family.

4. One Family, Two Houses

Looking at the Lee Dong-beom and Lee Jae-seung head family house and the Lee Jae-hyeok House, what I came to consider important is that the two houses must not be understood as completely separate histories. Both houses begin from a common family line: Seok-am Lee Dong-beom and his two sons.

The 1917 Maeil Sinbo shows the relationship between Lee Dong-beom and his two sons, and the Hampyeong Lee Clan Comprehensive Genealogy supports that lineage. Baekbeom Ilji also records the story of the brothers Lee Jae-seung and Lee Jae-hyeok going to see Kim Koo after liberation (Part 3).[Note]

In this way, the history of the two brothers also appears together within the bond between Kim Koo and the Lee Dong-beom family. However, the historical fact that they belong to the same family and the question of whether the two houses were architecturally once a single space are separate matters. Whether the two houses were divided from one large house in the past, or whether living spaces were separated by building new structures, and how the inner quarters, sarangchae, and gate building were arranged in that process, are difficult to establish with the materials obtained so far.

That is why old cadastral maps and land registers, building records, and the structure of the existing buildings must be compared comprehensively. We hope to clarify more concretely the former layout of these two houses and how they changed. Only then can the records and traditions about the spaces where Kim Koo stayed be understood more accurately.

5. The House That Remains

When we began this research, one of the things my father and I regretted most was that the existence of the Lee Dong-beom head family house did not come through sufficiently in some later records. The record of a particular building's demolition and the question of whether the head family house as a whole survives must be distinguished.

Even if part of the main house was demolished at some point, that alone does not mean a family's head family house has disappeared. Nor does the designation of one house as a cultural heritage deny the historical value of other buildings belonging to the same family.

Today the Lee Jae-hyeok House is known as a cultural heritage, but the head family house that continued through the line of the eldest son, Lee Jae-seung, also actually remains. And in that head family house are preserved old portrait photographs and documents, family heirlooms, and memories handed down through the generations.

Among them is the original Gwangmyeong calligraphy, introduced in Part 3, handed down as the work of Kim Koo and bearing a date in 1947. What we hope for is not to set the two houses against each other or to deny the value of one in order to emphasize the other.

It is to restore fully the history of the Lee Dong-beom family of Hampyeong, which has not been sufficiently known, by looking at the Lee Dong-beom and Lee Jae-seung head family house and the Lee Jae-hyeok House together.

6. The Next Step

2026 marks the 150th anniversary of Kim Koo's birth. Prompted by the Kim Koo Museum & Library's interview with my father this year, our family too has begun to organize more systematically the stories and materials handed down to us. And in November 2026, we asked Hampyeong County to conduct an on-site survey and to review preservation measures, so that the historical and cultural heritage value of the main building of the Lee Dong-beom head family house in Hampyeong can be examined.

In that process, we hope that the relationship between the present head family house and the Lee Jae-hyeok House, the location of the old hexagonal pavilion, the history of the attic and the dugout, and the construction dates of the buildings will be confirmed objectively. A request for an on-site survey does not mean cultural heritage designation or the establishment of historical fact.

Rather, it is a first step toward carrying the questions that must now be answered into official surveys and records. I believe history does not end with finding old records. It requires visiting the places the records point to, examining the buildings that still remain, and listening together to the memories of the people who have lived in them.

Our family's research, which began with one small passage in Baekbeom Ilji, now extends to looking together at Lee Dong-beom and his two sons, the head family house and the Lee Jae-hyeok House, and the modern history of Hampyeong. The exact appearance of the house where Kim Koo stayed 128 years ago has yet to be clarified. But there are descendants who have kept the head family house in memory of that bond, and there are documents, heirlooms, and spaces that remain today through which that history can be confirmed.

We will continue to record confirmed facts accurately and to keep researching what has not yet come to light.

To look again at a part of history that has not been sufficiently known, and to record properly the value of the spaces that still remain.

That is why my father and I are continuing this research.
