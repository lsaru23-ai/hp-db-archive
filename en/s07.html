[Back Through Time] ⑥ Lee Don-hwa (Don-baek) — The Manho of Dagyeong-po and Two Official Orders

0001①②③④⑤⑥⑦

Before we begin

The sixth detailed chronology is about my seventh-generation ancestor Lee Don-hwa (李敦華, 1797–1860). The genealogy records that he served as manho (萬戶, commander of a small naval garrison) of Dagyeong Garrison in the 7th year of King Cheoljong (1856). In the same year the Seungjeongwon Ilgi records the appointment of "Lee Don-baek (李敦白)" as manho of Dagyeong-po, and two official orders (jeollyeong) addressed to the same name survive in the family.

The family has long held that Lee Don-baek and Lee Don-hwa are the same person. This post sets out how the genealogy, the state record and the family documents overlap, and what is still missing between the two names.

2. What the records show

Three kinds of record overlap in 1856. The genealogy says Lee Don-hwa served as manho of Dagyeong that year; the Seungjeongwon Ilgi records Lee Don-baek's appointment as manho of Dagyeong-po the same year; and the family holds orders addressed to Lee Don-baek, manho of Dagyeong-po. The matching post and year, and the fact that documents addressed to the man in the state record have come down in this family, support the family tradition.

The two orders are separate documents, dated the 8th month of 1856 and the 1st month of 1858. A successor was appointed in September 1858.

3. What the records do not show

The relationship between Lee Don-baek and Lee Don-hwa

We have not yet found a document that states directly that the two names refer to the same person. On the original page of the Seungjeongwon Ilgi the character 白 is small but unmistakably 白. The present judgment rests on the family tradition, the matching post and year, and the fact that the documents came down in the family.

Length of service

Receiving orders in the 8th month of 1856 and the 1st month of 1858 does not mean he served continuously in between. We have not established when or why he left the post.

Details of the orders

The administrative meaning of 「自望」, the characters of the seal, and the "中軍" mark have not yet been read.

The inscription stone

We have not seen the inscription stone recorded in the genealogy or established when the pavilion was built, and we do not yet know whether the name 敦白 also appears on the stone.

4. Documents that kept a name

The two orders have been kept in the family for generations. Because they are documents held by the family, this series does not publish photographs of them; only the readable parts are presented in Archive Record 022. Two sheets of paper, kept in the family for nearly 170 years, have become the most important thread linking the "Lee Don-baek" of the state record and the "Lee Don-hwa" of the genealogy.

Closing

My seventh-generation ancestor's chronology remains a question about one name. Answering it will require finding the two names written together, on the inscription stone or in another official record.
