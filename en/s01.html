[Back Through Time] Seven Generations in One Post — From Lee Bung-seo to Lee Sang-rae

0001①②③④⑤⑥⑦

Before we begin

As explained in the previous post, "How We Correct the Record," the new series opens with a short chronology covering all seven generations in one post. It follows about 190 years in chronological order, from 1779 to 1970, from my eighth-generation ancestor Lee Bung-seo to my grandfather Lee Sang-rae.

From the next post onward, each person's story will be told in detail in reverse order, beginning with my grandfather and moving back one generation at a time. Please think of this post as the map to unfold before reading those seven parts.

How to read this post

The mark at the end of each entry shows which kind of record it comes from, because records carry different weight.

[G] Records made by the government or public offices (Seungjeongwon Ilgi, Official Gazette, family register, etc.)

[F] Original documents and objects held by the family (examination diploma, official orders, school diploma, calligraphy, etc.)

[Gen] Hampyeong Lee Clan Comprehensive Genealogy

[N] Newspaper articles of the time

[L] Later compilations, biographical directories, memoirs and survey reports (including Baekbeom Ilji)

[O] Oral tradition handed down in the family

Baekbeom Ilji is the memoir written by Kim Gu himself, and it is the most direct record of his 1898 stay in Hampyeong and the 1946 reunion, written by the person who lived them. Because it was written years after the events, however, names and dates in it are read against other records.

Where no record was found, we say only that we "could not confirm" it. The absence of a record does not mean that something did not happen.

1. Lee Bung-seo (李鵬緖, 1779–1809) — 24th generation, 8th-generation ancestor

No office is recorded for him in the genealogy. According to the genealogy, his son Lee Don-hwa composed his tomb inscription (myogal, the text on the stele before the grave). [Gen]

The family's records begin with him, but we have not yet searched for government records of his life.

2. Lee Don-hwa (李敦華, 1797–1860) — 25th generation, 7th-generation ancestor

The genealogy records that he served as manho (萬戶, commander of a small naval garrison) of Dagyeong Garrison (多慶鎭萬戶) in the 7th year of King Cheoljong (1856). [Gen]

In the same year the Seungjeongwon Ilgi records the appointment of "Lee Don-baek (李敦白)" as manho of Dagyeong-po, and two official orders (jeollyeong) addressed to the same name, dated 1856 and 1858, survive in the family. The family has long held that Lee Don-baek and Lee Don-hwa are the same person. We have not yet found a document that explains directly why the two names differ. [G][F] → Archive Record 022

His tomb inscription was composed by his grandson Lee Gye-song, and the stele pavilion was built by his great-grandson Lee Dong-beom. [Gen]

3. Lee Gyeong-heon (李警憲, 1825–1855) — 26th generation, 6th-generation ancestor

The genealogy records jobong daebu (朝奉大夫, a civil rank of the junior fourth grade) and gyogwan (敎官), as well as gyeongyeon chamchan (經筵叅贊), seungji (承旨) and myeongjeong (命旌). When and how he received these ranks and titles has not yet been established. [Gen]

In 1891, 36 years after his death, the court posthumously granted him the title of dongmong gyogwan for outstanding filial piety. This dongmong gyogwan was conferred after his death. How it relates to the jobong daebu rank recorded in the genealogy has not yet been established. [G] → Archive Record 024

4. Lee Gye-song (李啓松, 1841–1884), also named Lee Hong-gyu (李弘奎) — 27th generation, 5th-generation ancestor

He lost his father Lee Gyeong-heon at a young age. A diploma (hongpae) survives in the family showing that he passed the military examination in 1871, second place in the eulgwa class. [F]

From 1871 to 1883 the Seungjeongwon Ilgi records several appointments and replacements of an owijang (五衛將, a commander in the Five Guards) of the same name. Because no place is given, we only infer that he is likely the same person, without confirming it. The 1877 record, however, names the place: "Lee Hong-gyu, former owijang of Hampyeong," contributed 1,500 nyang to famine relief. [G] → Archive Record 023

His gravestone is inscribed with the titles jeolchung janggun and cheomji jungchubusa. He is also the one who composed the tomb inscription of his grandfather Lee Don-hwa. [Gen]

5. Seok-am Lee Dong-beom (石庵 李東範, 1869–1940) — 28th generation, great-great-grandfather

He is known as "Jinsa Lee" in Baekbeom Ilji.

1897: Appointed chambong (參奉, a junior post at a royal shrine or tomb) of Jogyeongmyo shrine and resigned of his own accord six days later. [G] → Archive Record 001

1898: The young Kim Gu, who had escaped from Incheon Prison, stayed in this house for about two weeks. Baekbeom Ilji records the two sons who were studying in the house. [L] → Series Part 2

1899: His name appeared as "former chambong" in a notice in the Hwangseong Sinmun. [N] → Archive Record 002

1902: Appointed councillor of the Jungchuwon and resigned the next day; appointed magistrate of Mangyeong later the same year and took office. [G] → Archive Record 003

1903: Resigned as magistrate of Mangyeong and was appointed magistrate of Eunyul in Hwanghae Province the next day. [G] → Archive Record 004

1913: Joseon Sinsa Daedongbo wrote that as magistrate of Mangyeong and Eunyul he was "upright and honest, leaving much goodwill behind." [L] → Archive Record 026

1917: The Maeil Sinbo introduced him as "a notable of South Jeolla," writing that he had given up office to care for his parents and returned home. [N] → Series Part 5

1937: Later records state that he built the Sesimjeong pavilion (洗心亭) in Hampyeong Park. [L]

The genealogy and records of 1913 and 1917 describe him as of the senior third rank (tongjeong daebu). How he received the rank is still being verified (see the footnote to Archive Record 003). He died in 1940. [Gen]

6. Chun-gang Lee Jae-seung (春崗 李載昇, 1890–1962) — 29th generation, great-grandfather

The eldest son of Lee Dong-beom, he succeeded as head of the main family line.

1898: Around the age of eight, he was one of the brothers studying in the house where Kim Gu stayed. [L] Baekbeom Ilji

1904: Appointed chambong of Sunneung royal tomb and left the post two days later. [G] → Archive Record 025

1913: Recorded as a school affairs committee member of Hampyeong Public Common School. [L] → Archive Record 026

1923: Named in a newspaper as head of the Hampyeong Heungsan Cooperative, and elected custodian (bogwan wiwon) of the Hampyeong branch of the People's University Movement the same year. [N] → Archive Record 008

1925: Named in a newspaper as an adviser (sangdamyeok) of Hampyeong Heungsan Co., Ltd. [N] → Archive Record 009

1938: The Dong-A Ilbo profiled him under the title 「古道에 充實 春崗 李載昇氏」, describing him as a former head of Hampyeong township. The dates of his service are not confirmed by records. [N] → Archive Record 011

1946: Visited Kim Gu in Seoul together with his younger brother Lee Jae-hyeok. [L] Baekbeom Ilji → Series Part 3

His dates follow the family register. [G] The genealogy gives his year of death as 1959. [Gen]

His younger brother Lee Jae-hyeok (李載爀) was born in 1893; the original text of Baekbeom Ilji writes his name as 「在赫」. He served as chairman of the executive committee of the Hampyeong branch of the People's University Movement in 1923, and visited Kim Gu with his elder brother in 1946. The "Lee Jae-hyeok House" in Hampyeong is designated a cultural heritage material of South Jeolla Province. The two brothers' story is told together in the part on Lee Jae-seung.

7. Lee Sang-rae (李祥來, 1909–1970) — 30th generation, grandfather

The eldest son of Lee Jae-seung, and my grandfather.

1909: Born on 8 January, according to his Jungdong School diploma. [F]

1930: Graduated from Jungdong School in Seoul. [F]

1930s: According to family tradition, he then graduated from Jungang Buddhist College, a predecessor of today's Dongguk University. The year has not been confirmed. [O]

Early 1940s: According to the memoir of his younger sister Lee Hak, he was taken to the police station during the period of compulsory grain deliveries for hiding rice set aside for the family, and released after about two weeks. The year and the outcome have not been confirmed in official documents. [L] Lee Hak's memoir → Series Part 7

1947: The name 「祥來」 is written on Kim Gu's calligraphy 「光明」 ("Light"), whose inscription is dated 23 June. How he received it has not been confirmed. [F] → Series Part 3

No record of an official post has been found within the scope of research so far. His dates follow the family register [G]; the genealogy records his death in the first lunar month of 1969. [Gen]

What the seven generations show

Put together, the family's records fall into roughly three layers. Up to the mid-19th century there are only the genealogy, the Seungjeongwon Ilgi and a few documents kept by the family. In the time of my great-great-grandfather Lee Dong-beom, the Official Gazette and newspapers appear and the record suddenly becomes dense. In the generations of my great-grandfather and grandfather, newspaper articles, family memoirs and records of the bond with Kim Gu are added.

The gaps are also clear. The lives of Lee Bung-seo and Lee Gyeong-heon, the relationship between the names Lee Don-baek and Lee Don-hwa, whether the owijang records really belong to Lee Gye-song, the dates of Lee Jae-seung's service as township head, and how Lee Sang-rae received the calligraphy have not yet been answered by records. Some records are still under review.
