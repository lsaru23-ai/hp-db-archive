[Back Through Time] ⑤ Lee Gyeong-heon — A Short Life and a Posthumous Title 36 Years Later

0001①②③④⑤⑥⑦

Before we begin

The fifth detailed chronology is about my sixth-generation ancestor Lee Gyeong-heon (李警憲, 1825–1855). By the dates in the genealogy he lived a little over thirty years, and died while his son Lee Gye-song was still young.

His name first appears in a state record in 1891, 36 years after his death, when the court conferred an office on him for his filial piety. This post sets out what the genealogy records, what the state record shows, and what we could not find.

2. What the records show

The 1891 entry in the Seungjeongwon Ilgi makes two things clear.

First, the office was conferred after his death. The 1891 document refers to him as "the late scholar (故學生)." This is simply how the document names him, and we do not use it as evidence either way about whether he held office in life.

Second, the reason was filial piety: 「孝行卓異」, "filial conduct outstanding." Recalling that the 1917 newspaper singled out the filial piety of my great-great-grandfather Lee Dong-beom as the family's defining trait, it is worth noting that filial piety was spoken of again across the generations of this family.

3. What the records do not show

Office in life

We have not yet found a record of an appointment during his lifetime in the Seungjeongwon Ilgi or the Korean History Database. The genealogy, however, records jobong daebu, a rank of the junior fourth grade. The missing record does not mean he held no office, and the genealogy entry alone does not confirm that he actually received the rank in life.

Ranks and titles in the genealogy

Besides jobong daebu and gyogwan, the genealogy records gyeongyeon chamchan (a post attending the royal lectures on the classics), seungji (a senior third-grade royal secretary) and myeongjeong (an order to erect a memorial gate honoring his conduct). We have not yet found contemporary government records that support these entries, nor established whether they were held in life or conferred after death, or whether the memorial gate was actually ordered.

We have not established whether the genealogy's "敎官" refers to the 1891 posthumous title (dongmong gyogwan), or whether "朝奉大夫" was a rank held in life or conferred after death. How the 1891 posthumous title relates to the jobong daebu rank in the genealogy has also not been established.

What the filial conduct was

The record does not say what the filial conduct was, or who reported it to the court and when. We plan to look for related memorials or local records.

4. What a short life left behind

Only three dates in his life are confirmed: his birth and death in the genealogy, and the 1891 posthumous title. Yet after that short life his son Lee Gye-song passed the military examination, his grandson Lee Dong-beom served as a magistrate, and the filial piety that earned him the posthumous title was spoken of again in a newspaper in his grandson's time.

Closing

Few records do not mean a small life. In his case, the clearest record was written a generation after his death. This post writes only as much as that record says.
