Before we begin

The first detailed chronology is about the nearest generation, my grandfather Lee Sang-rae (李祥來, 1909–1970). I told his story once before, in Part 7 of the earlier series, "Lee Sang-rae — The Grandson Whose Name Remains on 「光明」." This time I lay that story out again year by year, and show for each entry which record it rests on.

Sources: [G] government and public records (including the family register) · [F] originals and objects held by the family · [Gen] genealogy · [N] newspapers of the time · [L] later compilations, memoirs and survey reports (including Baekbeom Ilji) · [O] oral family tradition

Baekbeom Ilji is the memoir written by Kim Gu himself, and it is the most direct record of his 1898 stay in Hampyeong and the 1946 reunion, written by the person who lived them. Because it was written years after the events, names and dates in it are read against other records.

2. What the records show

Fewer dates in my grandfather's life are confirmed by documents than one might expect. His Jungdong School diploma gives his date of birth and the date he completed school. The 1947 date and the name 「祥來」 can be read on the 「光明」 calligraphy itself. The year of his death is written in the family register.

No record of an official post has been found within the scope of research so far. The material confirmed for my grandfather centres on a school diploma and family records rather than official records.

3. What the records do not show

The early-1940s detention: This episode survives only in the memoir of his younger sister Lee Hak. The exact year, the measure taken at the police station, and how long he stayed at Bulgapsa have not been confirmed in documents of the time. We plan to compare it with records on grain deliveries and the police in the Hampyeong area.

How he received the 「光明」 calligraphy: That the calligraphy bears his name and a date is a different matter from when, where and how Kim Gu gave it to him. How he received it has not yet been confirmed.

Authentication of the calligraphy: No independent expert examination of the brushwork and seal impression has yet been carried out. This task remains separate from the question of how he received it.

The 1930s: We have not yet found records of what he did between leaving Jungdong School and the early 1940s.

Year of death: The family register gives 1970 and the genealogy the first lunar month of 1969. This series follows the family register and notes both.

4. His place in the family

My grandfather was the eldest grandson of the main family line. The story of his wife Shin Mun-ran is in Series Part 8, and the content of the memoir left by his younger sister Lee Hak is in Series Part 7. Lee Ok, remembered in the family as another younger sister, received a Presidential Commendation in 2022 for her role in the Gwangju Student Independence Movement.

Closing

Laying out my grandfather's life as a chronology, the time we could not confirm turned out to be longer than the dates we could. Rather than fill those gaps with guesses, I have written only as far as the records reach.

The next part looks at the chronology of his father, my great-grandfather Chun-gang Lee Jae-seung, together with the story of his younger brother Lee Jae-hyeok.
